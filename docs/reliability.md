# Reliability and cancellation

Task phases are explicit: queued, waiting for worker, downloading, download complete, waiting for processor, converting/merging, completed; retry waiting, paused, cancelling, cancelled, failed and interrupted represent exceptional/control paths. Domain transitions reject illegal advancement. Completion cannot resurrect a paused or cancelled task. Resume waits until the previous running worker has exited.

Whole-job retries belong to the scheduler. Errors are classified as invalid URL, unavailable media, network, rate limit, disk full, permission failure, process startup, yt-dlp, FFmpeg, cancellation, timeout, corrupt output, or unknown. Network/timeouts/rate limits retry up to the configured count; unavailable/processing failures receive at most one bounded retry. Validation, permission, disk, cancellation and corrupt-output errors require intervention. Backoff is exponential with jitter, capped at five minutes; rate-limit retries start no earlier than 15 seconds and host cooldown may postpone them further. Engine retries and fragment retries are separately short and finite.

One item's failure does not stop the playlist. Canonical identities ignore YouTube timestamps/list context and include format/quality/container. Equivalent queued/saved items are suppressed unless Rename explicitly requests another copy. Suppressed playlist entries are recorded as cancelled duplicates with a reason, not downloaded again. Playlist numbering is retained even when an item fails, is suppressed, or receives a different priority.

Subprocesses launch with argument arrays, hidden windows and kill-on-drop. On Windows, creation is suspended until the PID is attached to a kill-on-close Job Object; only then is its thread resumed. Descendants inherit the Job Object. Cancellation, timeout, worker destruction and shutdown close the process tree rather than leaving FFmpeg or yt-dlp descendants alive. Streams have hard line limits, stderr is bounded, downloaded result paths are bounded and canonicalized, and overall process timeouts apply.

Final media is not published just because a child exits successfully. FFprobe verifies required audio/video streams and a finite positive duration before atomic publication. This is structural media validation, not an exhaustive decode of every frame. ZIP creation writes a partial archive, flushes it, rechecks cancellation, then publishes it. Cancellation/failure removes incomplete ZIPs. Optional source-folder removal occurs only after a successful complete archive.

Shutdown first closes admission and cancels all active phases, then joins worker tasks against one shared five-second deadline and aborts any remaining async handles. Partial download data and staged media remain available for a subsequent explicit resume. Installer signing, all external media-player associations and every possible YouTube restriction are outside the offline test guarantees.
